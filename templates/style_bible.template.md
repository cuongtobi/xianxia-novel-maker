# 4. High-Level Calibration Traits

These are descriptive tendencies, not per-paragraph rules:

- prose advances through cause, reaction, consequence and next action;
- reasoning appears when it changes choice or tactic;
- technical exposition is tied to a live object/problem and changes risk/value/option;
- combat remains spatially readable and escalates through counters, revealed capability, injury, resource, terrain or position;
- dialogue is practical and status-aware;
- narrator may briefly zoom out for world consequence, then return to concrete local action;
- simple direct emotional labels are allowed when natural;
- do not manufacture literary punchlines or aphorisms.

# 5. Translation / Convert Filter

Do not inherit from external calibration samples:

- Chinese clause order in Vietnamese;
- punctuation/spacing errors;
- repeated connector tics;
- awkward pronoun repetition;
- repeated superlative/exclamation patterns;
- spectator reaction after every combat exchange;
- onomatopoeia spam;
- source-specific terminology, identities, plot or cultivation design.

# 6. Originality Contract

Examples may calibrate:

- rhythm;
- narrative distance;
- dialogue texture;
- descriptive density;
- action/exposition balance;
- paragraph movement;
- degree of explicitness;
- causal scene flow.

Examples may **not** be copied for:

- wording;
- distinctive imagery/metaphor;
- rhetorical frames;
- paragraph sequence;
- scene structure;
- plot beats;
- character/world identity.

# 7. Prose Edit Boundary

The editor should prefer leaving good prose untouched.

Permitted local improvements:

- remove explanation already demonstrated by scene;
- compress report-like reasoning without deleting decision-relevant thought;
- repair unnatural Vietnamese / convert-like clause order;
- trim obvious repetition;
- improve local causal/spatial clarity.

Do not standardize cadence or paragraph length. Do not manufacture fragments, aphorisms or punchlines.
